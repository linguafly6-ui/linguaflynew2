# Pilot source extraction — Pre-A1 001 and A1 001 (v1)

**Status:** source inspection and extraction map only. Neither mission is converted or certified against the master schema.  
**Source baseline:** `main` at the time of inspection; the extraction branch is chained from validator-test commit `28c1ce3eb01f921ef7d0e2690796a66743a180b6`.  
**Language target:** `en-GB`.  
**Important:** this document records existing source material and gaps. It does not create replacement exercises, answer keys, audio assets, or stable production IDs.

## 1. Executive result

The two missions contain useful recoverable material, but neither is ready to become a canonical v1 mission JSON without editorial decisions and additional source work.

- **Pre-A1 001 — Greetings and Introductions:** Vocabulary, 50 Practice questions, 10 Grammar questions, 10 Pronunciation cards, a Reading passage with 10 questions, and a Listening script with 10 multiple-choice questions are present. The 22 Writing dictations are unrelated animal/nature vocabulary, and the Speaking UI is a word-selection recorder rather than ten independently authored prompts. Listening is a quiz, not the required inline gap-fill.
- **A1 001 — Who Am I?:** the central A1 lesson data provides 10 vocabulary words, a full Reading passage, a Listening script, writing/speaking prompts, and grammar focus. The runtime generates ten Grammar, Writing, Speaking and Listening tasks. Some are generated by cycling or sampling from a small source set, not authored as ten distinct items. Pronunciation cards are generated from the ten vocabulary words. Reading has a ten-question set. Listening already has inline gaps and a movable word bank, but its gaps are selected algorithmically from the script.
- Neither source provides the permanent hierarchical IDs required by the master contract. Existing local identifiers such as `W001-01` are not canonical IDs and must not be reused as final IDs without mapping.

### 2.3 Grammar

**Assessment:** count matches 10. The existing correct answers and distractors are available in source. They can be mapped into canonical prompt/evaluation objects after review.

### 2.4 Pronunciation

The `extraSections.pronunciation` array contains **10** word records: hello, goodbye, please, thank you, yes, no, name, friend, teacher and student. **Assessment:** count matches 10 and the displayed cards have cues. A clean translation mapping and reviewed cue transcription are needed before conversion. There are no verified audio-file URLs in this source.

### 2.5 Reading

The `extraSections.reading` array contains **10** multiple-choice questions about Alex, Mia, Ben, greetings and polite expressions.

### 2.6 Writing

### 2.7 Speaking

**Assessment:** the important distinction between transcription and phonetic evaluation is already stated. However, this is not ten separately identified prompts with model answers. Ten prompt/answer pairs and their stable IDs are an editorial gap. The server/API response and actual evaluation capability must be inspected separately before claiming pronunciation analysis.

### 2.8 Listening

The `extraSections.listening` array contains **10** multiple-choice comprehension questions. The transcript is hidden until the learner finishes the questions. Playback is provided through the page's speech/audio implementation; no stable audio asset URL is declared in the mission data.

**Assessment:** transcript and question count exist, but the interaction is not the required gap-fill. A new ten-gap transcript template and ten-word bank must be authored and checked against the audio/script. Do not treat the ten multiple-choice answers as gap-fill answer keys.

## 3. A1 001 — exact recoverable source

**Sources:** `public/missions/a1/001/index.html` and `public/missions/a1/a1-first-10-sections.js` on `main`.

The page loads the shared script. The script selects lesson data from the URL path, using lesson key `1`.

### 3.1 Vocabulary / Exploración

Lesson title: **Who Am I?**

The lesson's ten words are:

`name`, `surname`, `age`, `address`, `city`, `country`, `nationality`, `phone number`, `email address`, `date of birth`.

**Assessment:** ten words are present. The runtime derives Spanish meanings through `translateWord`; the exact mapping must be carried over and reviewed rather than guessed from the visible English list. The runtime also appends a grammar-theory card to the exploration panel, which is a presentation choice, not a separate canonical section.

### 3.2 Practice

The A1 001 page has its legacy Practice quiz with **50** questions and a 50-question score/progress display.

**Assessment:** the master contract requires 20 mixed autocorrected tasks. A reviewed 20-item selection is required; do not mechanically truncate the 50-item list. The shared A1 lesson script does not turn that legacy Practice list into the new 20-item contract.

### 3.3 Grammar

Lesson grammar key: `be`. The rule is to use `am` with I, `is` with he/she/it, and `are` with you/we/they; negatives use `not`, and questions place the verb before the subject.

The source's three authored task templates and answers are:

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| I ___ from Madrid. | am |
| My surname ___ Brown. | is |
| ___ you a student? | Are |

The runtime creates ten autocorrected Grammar tasks by cycling these three templates (`i % g.tasks.length`).

**Assessment:** a ten-task UI exists, but it repeats three source tasks to fill ten positions. This does not meet the requirement for ten distinct, authored exercises. Seven additional distinct tasks, or an explicitly approved repetition policy, are needed. The page also renders theory, examples and a short production instruction; those do not substitute for ten assessed items.

### 3.4 Pronunciation

The runtime creates a card for each of the ten lesson vocabulary words. Each card displays the English word, a Spanish meaning from `translateWord`, an approximate cue from `pronunciationCue`, and a browser speech-synthesis button with language `en-GB`.

**Assessment:** ten cards are generated from the ten vocabulary words and have the required kinds of displayed information. No standalone audio asset is referenced; speech is synthesised in the browser. The exact cue/translation dictionaries should be exported with the data and reviewed before canonicalisation. The card section itself says there is no recording or phonetic evaluation there; oral practice is in Speaking.

### 3.5 Reading

The full source passage is:

> My name is Laura García. I am twenty-four years old. I am from Spain and I live in Madrid. My address is 14 Green Street. My phone number is 612 345 678. My email address is laura@example.com. My date of birth is 12 May.

The `readingQuestionSets[1]` source contains **10** questions with one answer and two distractors each. They ask about the main idea, Laura's age, country, city, surname, address, phone number, date of birth, a true statement and the purpose of an email address.

**Assessment:** passage and ten question records are available. The runtime renders the passage in a separate “Read the text” card before the question task UI. This matches the requested reading layout in principle. Verify the exact question/answer set during extraction; the runtime shuffles distractors for display.

### 3.6 Writing

The lesson-level `writing` field is an open-ended instruction to write a personal profile with name, age, country, city, address and contact details. The runtime separately generates ten dictation tasks: the first five targets are the first five vocabulary words; the remaining five targets are selected from sentences in the Listening script. Playback uses browser speech synthesis with `en-GB`; the learner types the target and the runtime checks a normalised text match.

**Assessment:** the runtime generates ten dictation interactions, but the targets are selected algorithmically rather than supplied as ten independently reviewed dictation records. The source has no persistent audio URLs or canonical expected-answer IDs. Ten explicit dictation items should be frozen as authored data before conversion.

### 3.7 Speaking

The lesson-level prompt asks the learner to introduce themselves to a new classmate, state name, age, nationality and city, and ask three questions. The runtime generates ten prompts from five prompt patterns and the ten vocabulary words. Each item has a target word as a model. The learner records audio; the code sends it to `/api/pronunciation/analyze` and displays transcription, a transcription-match score, feedback and British-English tips if returned.

The UI explicitly says the match score indicates which words Whisper recognised and **does not evaluate phoneme quality**.

**Assessment:** the recording/transcription flow is more developed than Pre-A1 001, and ten prompt interactions are generated. However, they are composed from five repeating prompt patterns, not ten individually authored model answers. Canonical data must separate `transcription_feedback` from any genuine `pronunciation_analysis`; do not label the transcription score as phonetic accuracy. Confirm the API implementation and its current model/response contract before representing the tips as an independently validated phonetic assessment.

### 3.8 Listening

The source Listening script is:

> Hello. My name is Daniel Ruiz. I am twenty-eight years old. I am Spanish. I live in Seville. My phone number is 600 123 456. My birthday is on 3 March.

The runtime extracts words from the script, selects ten word positions algorithmically, renders them as inline gaps, and shuffles the ten answers into a selectable bank. Learners can place, move or replace words, check their answers, and reveal solutions. Audio playback uses browser speech synthesis with `en-GB`.

**Assessment:** the interaction pattern is close to the master requirement: ten inline gaps, a word bank, movement/replacement, checking and solutions. But the gap selection is generated by sampling word positions from the transcript, not a reviewed ten-gap editorial exercise. Duplicate surface words may also arise if the sampled positions contain repeated words. Before conversion, explicitly define ten distinct bank entries with stable word IDs, ten corresponding gap tokens, and a transcript template that references each exactly once. There is no stable audio-file URL in the lesson data.

## 5. Canonicalisation blockers and decisions

1. **No canonical mission JSON is produced by this extraction.** Required content and evaluation details are not fully represented in source data, and the master schema intentionally rejects missing fields.
2. **No stable IDs exist for the legacy source items.** Generate IDs only after the final ten/twenty item selection is approved and frozen. Keep a separate old-to-new mapping for traceability.
3. **Audio strategy needs an explicit contract.** Current browser speech synthesis is not a stable audio asset. Decide whether the canonical `audio` field may represent a TTS instruction/configuration or must resolve to a stored audio asset. Do not write a fabricated URL.
4. **Answer keys need server-side protection.** Legacy correct answers are present in client-side JavaScript. The extraction report records their existence but does not copy them into a public mission payload.
5. **Editorial content gaps remain.** In particular, Pre-A1 001 Writing, Speaking and Listening need appropriate authored content/mechanics; A1 001 Grammar, Writing, Speaking and Listening need review and explicit item-level data.
6. **Runtime and persistence are out of scope.** This change does not modify page HTML, shared runtime, API, database, or student progress storage.

## 6. Recommended next phase

1. Approve the editorial source lists for the four incomplete areas: Pre-A1 001 Writing/Speaking/Listening and A1 001 Grammar/Listening (plus explicit itemisation of A1 Writing/Speaking).

5. Do not merge or deploy until explicitly authorised.
